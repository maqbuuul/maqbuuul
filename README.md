## About

I'm a **Customer & Marketing Data Engineer** working at the intersection of data engineering, marketing measurement, customer analytics, and revenue.

I started in advertising, where I learned a simple lesson:

> **Getting a click is easy. Proving what it produced is not.**

Today, I build the systems that close that gap — connecting advertising platforms, analytics, CRMs, operational systems, and payments into reliable data pipelines and models.

The problems I care about sit beyond the dashboard:

**Did the lead become a customer?  
Did the customer book?  
Did they show up?  
Did the transaction happen?  
And can we trace the revenue back to the acquisition source?**

**Ad click → Lead → Customer → Booking → Attendance → Revenue**

I build the data infrastructure that makes that chain measurable, testable, and defensible.

---

## What I build

- **Marketing data systems** — pipelines connecting advertising platforms, analytics, CRMs, and operational data.
- **Customer data platforms** — unified customer views, segmentation, cohorts, retention, churn, LTV, and behavioral analysis.
- **Marketing attribution** — measurement that follows customers beyond leads and platform-reported conversions.
- **Revenue data systems** — reconciliation across transactions, payments, bookings, orders, and operational records.
- **Analytics engineering** — warehouses, dbt models, transformations, testing, and business-ready datasets.
- **Predictive systems** — churn, LTV, segmentation, demand forecasting, stockout risk, and customer propensity.
- **Reverse ETL & activation** — moving warehouse-derived segments and predictions back into CRMs and marketing platforms.
- **Data products** — turning data pipelines and analysis into tools that people can use to make decisions.

## Engineering principles

### Follow the data to the outcome.

A marketing platform can tell you that a conversion happened.

A CRM can tell you that a booking was created.

A payment system can tell you that money was collected.

The interesting engineering problem is connecting those events into one trustworthy story.

I care about:

- **Traceability** — knowing where a number came from.
- **Reconciliation** — checking systems against each other instead of assuming they're correct.
- **Data quality** — making missing, duplicated, or inconsistent data visible.
- **Reproducibility** — being able to explain how a metric or decision was produced.
- **Automation** — removing repetitive manual work from the path between data and decisions.
- **Business context** — building systems around the decision they need to support, not the technology used to build them.

> **A booking isn't the product. A person walking through the door is.**

That principle shapes how I think about customer and marketing data.
